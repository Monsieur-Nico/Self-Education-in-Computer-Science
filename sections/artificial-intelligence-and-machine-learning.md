# Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

## What to Expect

This is a big, fast-moving section — it moves from classical algorithms that "learn" from data (regression, decision trees, clustering) up through neural networks and deep learning, and finishes at the transformer-based models behind today's large language models and generative AI. Unlike most sections, this one leans heavily on the math background from earlier in the curriculum: you'll be applying linear algebra, probability, and statistics constantly, not just occasionally.

Topics covered:

- Overview of artificial intelligence and machine learning
- Supervised learning algorithms (linear regression, logistic regression, decision trees, support vector machines, k-nearest neighbors, naive Bayes)
- Unsupervised learning algorithms (clustering, dimensionality reduction)
- Reinforcement learning
- Neural networks and deep learning
- Natural language processing
- Computer vision
- Transformers, large language models, and generative AI

The sub-estimates you'll see in some ML course syllabi (a few weeks per topic) add up fast — treat the section's overall length as "however long the topics above actually take you," not a fixed number.

## What to Prepare

**Prerequisites:** [Programming Fundamentals](/#programming-fundamentals-4-6-weeks), [Mathematics for Computer Science](/#mathematics-for-computer-science-8-12-weeks) — specifically linear algebra and probability/statistics. Without them, the material becomes memorization instead of understanding.

A few things worth knowing going in:

- **The math isn't optional background here — it's the material.** If linear algebra or probability feels shaky, it's worth pausing to reinforce it rather than pushing through the ML content on faith.
- **You'll want a way to actually run code with real datasets**, not just watch lectures — Python with NumPy/pandas (and a notebook environment like Jupyter) is the standard setup most courses assume.
- **This field moves fast.** The "Transformers, large language models, and generative AI" topic in particular covers ideas that are actively evolving — treat the fundamentals (how a transformer attends to context, what training actually does) as the durable part, and specific tools/models as things that will keep changing.

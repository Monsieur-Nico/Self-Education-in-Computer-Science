# Computer Architecture and Systems

## What to Expect

This section actually covers two related but distinct things: how a computer is physically built and organized (architecture — memory, I/O, how hardware and software meet), and how an operating system manages all of that on your behalf (processes, memory virtualization, file systems). It's "how it actually works under the hood" material, and it's noticeably more conceptual than the sections before it — expect to spend more time reading and reasoning than writing code, at least at first.

Topics covered:

- Overview of computer hardware and software
- Operating systems
- Memory management
- Input/output systems
- Computer networks
- Distributed systems

Computer networks and distributed systems get lighter treatment here than the other topics — they're touched on rather than covered in depth. That's a known gap we're planning to address with more dedicated material down the line.

## What to Prepare

**Prerequisites:** [Programming Fundamentals](../README.md#programming-fundamentals-4-6-weeks) — you don't need anything beyond solid basics; this section builds conceptual understanding more than it demands new coding skill.

A few things worth knowing going in:

- **The two Primary resources below cover different halves of this section**, not the same material twice — it's fine (and often better) to work through them in parallel rather than one after the other.
- **OSTEP includes hands-on projects** (in C) if you want to get your hands dirty with operating-systems concepts rather than just reading about them — optional, but worth doing if you have the time.
- **This is a good section to slow down in.** The ideas here (virtual memory, scheduling, how a network packet actually gets somewhere) tend to reward re-reading more than pushing straight through.
